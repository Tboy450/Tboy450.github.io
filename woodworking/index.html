Woodworking & Woodcraft

A growing woodcraft area of the HASK workshop. The current examples focus on laser-burned artwork on pine; the wider work also looks at how grain, ridges, surface preparation and finishing affect the final piece.

Current wood samples

Burned into the surface, not routed into it

These examples are surface burns made with the diode laser. They show image treatment, contrast and line detail on wood; they are not deep CNC-carved reliefs. Each piece is a useful record of how a design translated to the actual timber.

Designing for real wood

The grain and the surface are part of the design

A flat image preview cannot show every ridge, grain line or change in the timber. Artwork has to be judged against the actual board, not only the digital file.

Read the board

Record the species when known, grain direction, knots, ridges, dips and the usable flat area before deciding where the design belongs.

Prepare the surface

Check whether the piece is level and whether sanding or other preparation changes the way the mark appears. Keep a test area or offcut for comparisons.

Adapt the artwork

Choose line weight, shading and contrast for the grain and surface. A design that reads cleanly on a smooth area may need adjustment across uneven grain.

Evaluate the finish

Compare the burned result before and after any proposed finish. Record whether the finish changes contrast, residue, sheen or the apparent depth of the mark.

Workshop process

From selected timber to a documented piece

1. Select and inspect

Choose a board or offcut and note its surface condition, dimensions and grain.

2. Make a small test

Use a sample patch or a design detail to learn how this particular piece marks.

3. Prepare and burn

Set the artwork, focus and placement for the physical piece, then record the process used.

4. Review and finish

Inspect the result, remove loose residue as appropriate, and test finishing options on a separate sample before using them on a finished piece.

Finish order and stain compatibility are still being evaluated. This page does not present a stain-before or stain-after sequence as settled. The recorded result should decide the process.

Growing portfolio

Woodcraft alongside digital fabrication

Woodworking, surface art and laser work can share one design path without being treated as the same process. HASK can develop the digital artwork, make test pieces, and document the physical result; hand-finished and hand-painted work can be added as those examples are ready.
