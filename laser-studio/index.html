Laser Studio

A connected laser-workflow project: image preparation, the Laser Photo Studio application, positioning tools, material experiments, sample records and repeatable physical setup. The software and the machine are treated as separate parts of one working system.

The working sequence

Picture to checked job to physical sample

Laser Photo Studio is built around a five-step workflow. It prepares the image and job file, checks the generated commands offline, and leaves the final material result to be judged on the actual workpiece.

  1. Choose the pictureOpen a photo, drawing or logo; rotate or mirror it as needed.
  2. Choose material and styleUse a machine-tested preset, an external starting point, an experimental setting or a saved personal preset.
  3. Set size and placementChoose the workpiece, origin, margins, no-burn zones and small position adjustments.
  4. Preview and verifyAdjust tone and detail, inspect the output, then replay every command through the offline checks.
  5. Position and runSet the origin, use the 1% dot perimeter check, and start the job through the controller workflow.

The software layer

What Laser Photo Studio handles

Material-specific presets

Presets distinguish settings proven on the U20 from external starting points, experimental options and user-saved presets. Each can carry its explanation, matching LaserGRBL settings and supporting sample photos.

Image-to-mark styles

Layered shading, error-diffusion dots, ordered grids, halftone, grayscale power, black-and-white, thin-line and pencil-sketch treatments turn artwork into different engraving patterns.

Workpiece geometry

Set the piece dimensions and origin, preserve margins and no-burn zones, and nudge artwork in measured increments. This is the software side of repeatable placement.

Offline job checks

The job is inspected before it reaches the laser: command ranges, burn area, duplicate dots and laser-off starts and ends are checked against the generated file.

Positioning and perimeter check

The companion positioning workflow uses a 1% dot. The perimeter routine checks the work area at its corners, can add edge points, then returns to the origin.

Run records and recovery

Jobs keep previews, settings, logs and photos. A USB interruption can be handled through a deliberate stop-point check and continuation from the next command, rather than blindly restarting the file.

The machine layer

Built around a Grbl diode workflow

The current application is documented for the Tronxy Ultrabot U20 and its Grbl-based controller. Its machine interface is designed around a known controller configuration and a controlled command path, not arbitrary G-code streaming.

Controller constraints

  • Does not home the machine.
  • Refuses controller setting changes through the job workflow.
  • Checks the expected laser and axis configuration before output.
  • Uses explicit laser-off commands at row and job boundaries.

Operator-visible run state

  • Shows layer progress, estimated time and head position.
  • Can park between layers for a progress photo.
  • Provides pause, controlled stop and emergency-stop controls.
  • Does not automatically restart a job after a fault.

The software's checks describe the generated job and controller workflow. They do not certify a material, focus, fixture, ventilation setup or physical engraving result; those are recorded against the actual machine and sample.

Ongoing development

Where the studio is being improved

Repeatable positioning

Connect the digital origin and perimeter preview to a physical jig so the workpiece can be centered and registered consistently.

Better material records

Keep the material, surface condition, focus, fixture, settings, passes, result photo and wipe/finish observations together.

Richer artwork preparation

Continue improving masks, grayscale and dither treatment, linework and relief-style outputs for different engraving jobs.

Wood-aware design

Account for grain direction, surface ridges and unevenness when artwork is sized and prepared for wood.
