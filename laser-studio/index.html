Laser Photo Studio

Custom engraving software by HASK, created to work around unclear controls, limited adjustment and misleading terminology in the existing laser-software workflow. It brings image preparation, placement, job checks and recorded experiments into one application.

The reason for the software

Understand the controls. Control the result.

Laser Photo Studio grew out of hands-on frustration with the existing LaserGRBL workflow: controls that did not explain their effects, too little useful control over the job, and technical labels that obscured what the machine would actually do. The goal is practical control with explanations tied to the picture, the material and the movement.

That work has produced a functioning desktop application: image treatments, saved material settings, placement previews, command checks, job records and run controls. The interface continues to be refined as real projects expose confusing labels or missing adjustments.

The working sequence

Picture to checked job to physical sample

Laser Photo Studio is built around a five-step workflow. It prepares the image and job file, checks the generated commands offline, and leaves the final material result to be judged on the actual workpiece.

  1. Choose the pictureOpen a photo, drawing or logo; rotate or mirror it as needed.
  2. Choose material and styleUse a machine-tested preset, an external starting point, an experimental setting or a saved personal preset.
  3. Set size and placementChoose the workpiece, origin, margins, no-burn zones and small position adjustments.
  4. Preview and verifyAdjust tone and detail, inspect the output, then replay every command through the offline checks.
  5. Position and runSet the origin, preview the selected placement check, confirm alignment, and start through the controller workflow.

The software layer

What Laser Photo Studio handles

Settings saved for the material

Presets distinguish settings with recorded U20 samples from external starting points, experimental options and user-saved presets. Each can carry its explanation, matching LaserGRBL settings and supporting sample photos. A sample supports its recorded setup, not every workpiece of the same material.

Choose how the image is marked

Layered shading, error-diffusion dots, ordered grids, halftone, grayscale power, black-and-white, thin-line and pencil-sketch treatments turn artwork into different engraving patterns.

Size, placement and margins

Set the piece dimensions and origin, preserve margins and no-burn zones, and nudge artwork in measured increments. This is the software side of repeatable placement.

Offline job checks

The job is inspected before it reaches the laser: command ranges, burn area, duplicate dots and laser-off starts and ends are checked against the generated file.

Positioning and perimeter check

The application offers a 1% alignment dot, footprint and outline checks, a 2% full burn trace, and a laser-off dry run. Each check has a preview; the operator confirms the selected path and placement before the job.

Run records and recovery

Jobs keep previews, settings, logs and photos. Recovery requires checking the saved job reference and physical alignment. A reset during queued motion leaves the stop point uncertain, so the last sent command cannot be assumed to be the last executed command.

Development record · November 17, 2026

More control, added through actual projects

These additions are implemented in the workshop application. They build on the original five-step workflow rather than remaining ideas for a future app.

Scan direction and return speed

Choose alternating rows or left-to-right marking. A separate laser-off return-speed control changes the movement between rows without changing engraving speed or power, and saves with the preset and job.

Saved setup and inspection moves

Keep the artwork and job setup available, park to inspect the piece and return to job zero. Physical alignment still needs checking after an interruption.

Saved USB-port choices

Keep known ports available and refresh device detection without silently switching the selection. This addresses using different USB sockets; it does not release another running sender.

Richer test and sample records

Record material, surface preparation, row spacing, passes and result status alongside settings and photos. Separate commanded speed from a measured or inferred result.

Verification and current limits

The November 9 investigation recorded 77 offline checks. Later port-selection and return-speed updates have their own regression checks; those counts describe different revisions, not one combined test total.

The live completion-time estimate has been reported to jump by hours and remains under investigation. Clearer Check-page explanations, native loading of prepared samples, project-size presets and release of another idle Studio connection remain unfinished.

Current material project · In development

Portrait artwork for slate coasters

The Artemis’s Place design extends the work from pine to a raw slate coaster. Preparation preserves the supplied portrait, dog, lettering and background detail while converting the image into a dot pattern intended to mark light on dark stone.

  • Artwork prepared: 89 × 89 mm for a measured coaster of approximately 100.6 × 100.6 mm.
  • Processing checked: Stucki dots, light-mark polarity and left-to-right scan preparation, with no extra sharpening added.
  • Physical assessment pending: finished contrast, small detail, placement and actual run time still need a reviewed sample record.
Recorded preparation and next checks

The prepared job uses 50% power, 3500 mm/min engraving and 0.10 mm rows as a starting point for this slate. A later setup adds an independently adjustable laser-off return speed. These are job-specific preparation values, not a calibrated recipe for all slate.

Placement has required adjustment on the actual coaster. The digital preview does not prove registration. Compare the finished piece with the dot map before drawing conclusions about likeness, eye detail, lettering or repeatability.

The machine layer

Current machine compatibility

The workshop installation connects to a Tronxy Ultrabot U20 using its Grbl controller. The connection checks and movement rules below describe that installation; the purpose of the application is to make engraving setup and control clearer.

Controller constraints

  • Does not home the machine.
  • Refuses controller setting changes through the job workflow.
  • Checks the expected laser and axis configuration before output.
  • Uses explicit laser-off commands at row and job boundaries.

Operator-visible run state

  • Shows layer progress, estimated time and head position.
  • Can park between layers for a progress photo.
  • Provides pause, controlled stop and emergency-stop controls.
  • Does not automatically restart a job after a fault.

The software's checks describe the generated job and controller workflow. They do not certify a material, focus, fixture, ventilation setup or physical engraving result; those are recorded against the actual machine and sample.

Ongoing development

Where the studio is being improved

Repeatable positioning

Connect the digital origin and perimeter preview to a physical jig so the workpiece can be centered and registered consistently.

Better material records

Keep the material, surface condition, focus, fixture, settings, passes, result photo and wipe/finish observations together.

Richer artwork preparation

Continue improving masks, grayscale and dither treatment, linework and relief-style outputs for different engraving jobs.

Clearer explanations and loading

Explain what Check-page controls actually change, load prepared projects with their complete settings, and add useful size presets.

A stable time estimate

Investigate the live estimate jumping by hours. Faster laser-off returns are implemented separately; their predicted savings are not measured run-time results.

Wood-aware design

Account for grain direction, surface ridges and unevenness when artwork is sized and prepared for wood.

Material-response ladders are a studio-wide research objective

A tighter ladder is not limited to the Red Hill tin. Laser Studio can use controlled power/speed or surface-response matrices to investigate separately identified materials, potentially including steel and titanium. Each material needs its own grade, surface/coating, thickness and suitability record; a result on one tin or metal must not be carried over as a universal preset.
